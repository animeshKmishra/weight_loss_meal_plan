What the plan answers

Each goal, and where the week takes care of it.

  • Lean muscle, defined groupsEvery muscle group is trained twice a week: legs on Monday and Thursday, chest, back and shoulders across Tuesday, Friday and Saturday. Sets of 5 to 15 reps close to failure, heavier every block. Supersets keep the rests short, so the lifting fits into 50 minutes.
  • Heart and lungsThe aerobic base comes first: three zone 2 finishers, Wednesday's 45 minutes and a brisk walk every evening. Intervals build through the block, from 1-minute to 3-minute efforts, and only move on when your heart-rate recovery says so.
  • EnergySupersets, carries, sleds and intervals keep the heart rate up all morning. The estimates on each day add up the minutes of each block at standard activity values; a watch will read differently.
  • Hips, hamstrings, lower backThree drills open every session. Hamstrings are loaded through their full length, hip flexors are stretched after every session, and the lower back is trained, not only stretched: McGill's three, carries, and rows with the chest supported.
  • Pelvic floorThe full set on both leg days, a 3-minute set on every other day, and breathing that doesn't strain it during heavy lifts.
  • Before breakfast, after a desk dayFasted training at 07:30 on one black coffee, salt water on waking, the shake at 09:00, and 2-minute movement breaks through the workday.

Adding weight

Every move has a rep range. When every set reaches the top of the range at the week's effort, add the smallest step next time: 2.5 kg on a barbell, 1 to 2 kg per dumbbell, one plate on a machine. After week 4, start again at week 1, using this block's week 2 weights as the new starting point.

Reading the numbers

Zones are estimates from a maximum of about 184 bpm, and formulas can be 10 beats off either way. If the talk test and the numbers disagree, trust the talk test. A chest strap reads more accurately than a wrist watch during intervals.

The recovery number

One minute after the last hard bout, note how far your heart rate has fallen. 15 beats or more: ready for the next interval stage. Fewer than 15: repeat the week. If it keeps falling by 12 or fewer, mention it to your doctor.

When easy feels hard

A heart rate that climbs quickly on easy work is common when you come back to cardio. It falls as the aerobic base returns, usually within 4 to 8 weeks of steady zone 2 work; the resting heart rate check tracks it.

Stop signs

  • Chest pain, pressure or tightness, or pain spreading to an arm, the jaw or the back.
  • Dizziness, light-headedness or feeling faint.
  • A heartbeat that feels irregular, skips or flutters.
  • A racing heart that doesn't settle below about 120 within 5 minutes of stopping.
  • Breathlessness far beyond what the effort explains.

Stop, sit down and tell someone nearby. Call 112 if chest pain lasts more than a few minutes or comes with sweating or breathlessness. See your doctor before the next hard session.

Hips, hamstrings and lower back

The fix is daily and spread across the week, not a stretching session once in a while.

  • Hip flexorsTight from sitting. The half-kneeling stretch opens every session, the couch stretch closes the leg days, and a 30-second version goes into the desk breaks.
  • Hip rotation90/90 switches every morning, the goblet squat pry on squat days, and a supported deep squat on Wednesday.
  • HamstringsStrap leg raises on Wednesday and Thursday. Romanian deadlifts and seated leg curls load them at full length, which is what builds range that lasts.
  • Lower backCat-cow every morning, McGill's three on Wednesday, bird dogs and dead bugs, rows with the chest supported, and a hang on Friday to decompress.
  • Inner thighsThe frog rock-back on Wednesday and the adduction machine on Thursday.

Pelvic floor

The full set closes both leg days; the short set fits into every other day. Letting go matters as much as the squeeze.

Finding the muscles

Lie on your back, knees bent. Breathe in and let the belly relax. Breathe out and gently lift and squeeze, as if holding in wind and urine at the same time. The lift is inward and upward; the buttocks, thighs and belly stay soft, and you keep breathing. Don't practise by stopping your urine flow.

The leg-day set, 5 minutes

  1. Slow holds: lift, hold for this week's time, then let go completely for 10 s. 10 times.
  2. Quick lifts: lift firmly for 1 s, let go for 1 s. 10 times.
  3. From week 3, the knack: lift just before you stand up from a chair or a squat. 5 times.
  4. Finish with 5 slow breaths, letting the pelvic floor soften fully on each breath in.

Holds and positions by week

  • Week 1. Lying on your back, knees bent: 10 slow lifts held 5 s with 10 s rest, then 10 quick 1 s lifts.
  • Week 2. Sitting upright: 10 slow lifts held 6 to 8 s with 10 s rest, then 10 quick lifts.
  • Week 3. Standing: 10 slow lifts held 8 to 10 s, 10 quick lifts, then 5 sit-to-stands, lifting just before you rise.
  • Week 4. Standing: 10 slow lifts held 10 s, 10 quick lifts, then 5 goblet squats, lifting just before each rise.

The daily set, 3 minutes

Every other day: one round of slow holds and one of quick lifts at this week's level, sitting at the desk or standing in the evening. Daily practice changes the muscle faster than twice a week.

Lifting with it

On heavy sets, brace and breathe out through the hardest part of the rep instead of holding your breath and pushing down. If heavy lifts bring on symptoms, lighten the load and raise it with a specialist.

Progress and help

Give it 8 to 12 weeks of daily practice; that's how long muscle strength takes to change. One early visit to a pelvic floor physiotherapist confirms you're working the right muscles. See a doctor if there's pain, or if nothing has improved by week 12.

Around the session

Fuel, the workday and sleep: the other 22.5 hours.

Before the session

On waking, about 06:45: 500 ml water with a pinch of salt and a squeeze of lemon. At 07:00, one cup of black coffee, and only one; skip pre-workout powders and energy drinks, which stack more stimulant on top. Sip 500 to 750 ml of water during the session.

Training before breakfast

Fasted training suits this plan: the shake straight after and breakfast at 09:30 start the recovery. If you feel dizzy or shaky, or your lifts drop two sessions running, have half a banana or 2 dates with the coffee.

Warm-up sets

Before each A lift, work up in three lighter sets: about half the work weight for 5 reps, then 70% for 3, then 85% for 1. They come out of the strength block's time.

The workday

Every 45 to 60 minutes, stand up for 2 minutes: 10 squats to your chair, the half-kneeling hip flexor stretch for 30 s a side, or a walk to refill your water. Take one call a day standing. Screen at eye level, feet flat, hips a little higher than the knees.

Sleep

Asleep by 23:00 for about 7.5 hours before a 06:30 alarm. Strength, appetite and resting heart rate all depend on it.

Your doctor

If you start a new medicine, or anything on the stop list happens, check with your doctor before the next hard session.
